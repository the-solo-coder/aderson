*<sub>Over the years, I’ve led teams ranging from 3 to 30 people. One of the smartest habits I developed as a manager? Keeping individual folders for each team member.</sub>*

*<sub>In those folders, I’d jot down both praise and opportunities for improvement. If I caught someone doing great work, I made sure to document it. I was always looking for moments to recognize their impact—but also to help them grow where they could improve.</sub>*

*<sub>That same mindset applies to your own career.</sub>*

*<sub>Today’s newsletter is your reminder to start doing this for yourself. Keep a simple log of your wins, lessons, and impact. When performance review season rolls around, you won’t have to scramble—you’ll just open your file and speak with confidence.</sub>*

*<sub>Enjoy,</sub>*

*<sub>— Aderson</sub>*

Ever walked out of a performance review thinking, “Wait… they didn’t mention half of what I did”? You’re not alone.

So here’s the real question: *If you don’t track your wins, how will anyone else remember them?*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Here’s what we’ll cover today:**

- 📸 **Capture the Small Wins Before They Disappear**

- 🧱 **Connect the Dots Between Work and Impact**

- 🧰 **Build a Ready-to-Go Toolkit for Performance Reviews**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 📸 Capture the Small Wins Before They Disappear

We forget 80% of what we do within days. That small thing you fixed? That meeting you led? That coworker you unblocked? Gone—unless you write it down.

Don’t wait for big milestones. Track tiny wins too. Here’s what qualifies as a “win”:

- You solved a recurring issue

- You made someone’s job easier

- You finished something before the deadline

- You received a nice Slack message or thank-you email

Set a reminder every Friday (or whatever cadence works for you) and jot it down:

- What was the task?

- What challenge did it solve?

- Who benefited?

Your future self—especially during review time—will thank you.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧱 Connect the Dots Between Work and Impact

Here’s the difference between “I worked on a feature” and “I improved our load time by 37%.” One sounds like a task. The other sounds like value.

Even if your role is behind-the-scenes or not easily measurable, you can still describe **impact**. Think:

- Did your work make something faster?

- Did it reduce errors?

- Did it improve team clarity, speed, or collaboration?

Don't just log *what* you did—log *why it mattered*. Managers don’t always have the context. That’s your job to bring into the light.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧰 Build a Ready-to-Go Toolkit for Performance Reviews

By keeping a consistent tracker, you're essentially building your own:

- Testimonial archive

- Case study library

- Promotion packet

When review time comes, you won’t be digging through months of memory. You’ll already have a highlight reel. You can even build themes around it:

- “Here’s how I contributed to stability”

- “Here’s how I collaborated across teams”

- “Here’s how I leveled up my skills this quarter”

And don’t wait until review season. Use this tracker in 1-on-1s, project retros, or even job interviews. It’s not about bragging—it’s about being visible.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Final Thoughts:

A Wins & Impact Tracker is not just a log. It’s a visibility strategy. It’s how you make sure your growth doesn’t stay in the shadows. It takes less than 10 minutes a week. But the return? Clarity, confidence, and better conversations.
