Today I bring to you error etiquette, coding for kids and when not to use pixels.

1.  [**Error messages best practices**](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hostile-error-messages/)

🛑 I never knew there was science behind proper error messages and how to make sure users of your system, site, app, don't feel stupid when using it.

This article talks about patterns of presenting messages that inform the user that something is not correct. For instance, **avoiding premature error** messages when filling out a postal code. Before the user has even a chance to finish the postal code, the form is already telling them that the code is incorrect.

Too much of anything is bad and error messages is no different.

2.  [**Coding for kids**](https://studio.code.org/projects/public)

🚸 This one comes a bit too late. Today Sunday Dec 11, is the last day of the [Annual Computer Science Education Week](https://www.csedweek.org/).

This is an initiative dedicated to **inspiring** K-12 (Kindergarten to Grade 12) **students to learn Computer Science**. It doesn't mean they will be coders, but coding is good to help developing mathematical, logical, and structural thinking, amongst many other skills.

I know you are not 12 years old, but you may have a sibling, a cousin, a neighbor, or maybe you are a parent, or an uncle/aunt. I have exposed my older daughter to coding early on and I'm glad I did it. Although her interest in coding didn't persist, but she enjoyed the experience. I want to do the same for the younger one.

If nothing else, a great tool for kids to try coding a bit in an easy, fun and free way is [Scratch](https://scratch.mit.edu/).[  
    ](https://martinfowler.com/tags/domain%20driven%20design.html%5D.￼)

3.  [**Never use Pixels to set font-size**](https://joshcollinsworth.com/blog/never-use-px-for-font-size)

📐 While the title is a bit exaggerated, I know that using pixels will limit how scalable your fonts are and **affect accessibility** as well.

This article promotes the use of EM and REM units (not to be confused with [R.E.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.M.)) instead of PX (for pixels).

I'm guilty of this. I don't think much about it when I need to do some CSS, but it doesn't mean that I don't know about it.
